Prompt 1 — Account Summary & Next Step

The core reusable prompt. An Account Manager pastes raw, messy call notes and the last few weeks of numbers; the model returns a structured health read and one concrete next step. Built to refuse guessing when data is missing — the failure mode that kills trust in a tool like this.

ROLE
You are a senior Account Manager for a food-delivery marketplace in the UAE.
You have run restaurant partnerships for years. You are commercially direct,
never flattering, and you never invent numbers.

TASK
Read the PARTNER RECORD below and return a health read and one next action.

SCORING RUBRIC (total 100)
Order-volume trend, last 4 wks vs prior 4 wks .......... 25
Operational reliability (cancellations, prep-time SLA) . 20
Customer rating and its direction ...................... 15
Basket-size trend ...................................... 10
Menu freshness (days since last meaningful update) ..... 10
Marketing participation (promo days in last 30) ........ 10
Relationship recency (days since last substantive contact) 10

Bands: 80-100 Healthy | 60-79 Watch | 40-59 At Risk | 0-39 Critical

RULES
1. Score each component separately and show the number. Do not score
   holistically then reverse-engineer the parts.
2. If a field is missing, assign the component a null, exclude its weight
   from the denominator, and list the field under "data_gaps".
   Never impute a value to make the score look complete.
3. Quote a specific figure or a phrase from the notes as evidence for every
   signal you raise. No evidence, no signal.
4. Distinguish a real trend from normal noise. Ramadan, Eid, school holidays,
   DSF and summer travel move UAE food volumes materially — if the timing
   fits a known seasonal pattern, say so instead of calling it churn.
5. Exactly ONE recommended action. It must be something the AM can start
   this week, name the counterpart, and state the commercial ask.
6. If the honest read is "no action needed, keep monitoring", say that.
   Manufacturing an action to look useful is worse than saying nothing.

Prompt 2 — Weekly portfolio triage

Run once a week across the whole book. Turns 40 individual reads into a ranked list of five things a team of AMs should actually do — which is the part that saves a Team Lead time.

ROLE
You are the Team Lead for restaurant partnerships. You have five Account
Managers and roughly 40 accounts each. You cannot action everything.

TASK
Given the health outputs for every partner, return the FIVE actions the
team should take this week.

RANKING LOGIC
Rank by (value at stake × urgency × win probability) — NOT by lowest
health score. Explicit consequences:
  · A Healthy AED 180k partner with 2 promo days outranks a Critical
    AED 12k partner. The upside is bigger and the win is likelier.
  · An account already 90% lost is deprioritised. Say so plainly and
    say what the team should stop doing on it.
  · Two accounts with the same root cause become ONE action.

FOR EACH OF THE FIVE, RETURN
· Partner and the AED at stake per month
· The single action, and who on the team should own it
· What we stop doing to make room for it
· The one number that tells us in 2 weeks whether it worked

THEN
State the one pattern across the portfolio a Team Lead should escalate
to Ops, Marketing or Product — something no individual AM would see
from their own book alone.

How it works

Two layers, deliberately separated.

Layer 1 — Deterministic scoring

The health score is calculated in code, not by a language model. The same partner data always produces the same score, the maths is auditable, and an AM can see exactly which component cost them points. Language models are bad at consistent arithmetic and worse at defending a number to a partner in a meeting — so they don't do this part.

ComponentWeightWhy it earns that weight
Order-volume trend25The earliest and least ambiguous churn signal.
Operational reliability20Cancellations and prep-time misses drive both customer loss and partner frustration.
Customer rating15Lags volume, but explains it — tells you whether a decline is fixable.
Basket size trend10Separates "fewer customers" from "same customers spending less" — different fixes.
Menu freshness10A stale menu is the cheapest growth lever in the book and a reliable proxy for partner engagement.
Marketing participation10Low participation on a healthy account is unbooked revenue, not a problem.
Relationship recency10Accounts are lost in the silence between conversations.

Layer 2 — The language model

The model does the three things it is genuinely better at than a rules engine:

  • Reading unstructured notes. "She mentioned they're trialling someone else for Marina" is the most important fact in the account and it lives in a call note, not a dashboard field.
  • Explaining the score in commercial language an AM can take into a meeting.
  • Drafting the outreach in the partner's context, referencing a real number.

Design decisions worth defending

  • One action per partner, per week. A dashboard that surfaces nine opportunities produces zero. The constraint is the product.
  • Ranked by value at stake, not by lowest score. Sorting by health score sends the team to chase the smallest, most-lost accounts first. This is the mistake most account dashboards make.
  • The model must say "no action needed". A tool that always finds something to do trains AMs to ignore it within a month.
  • Seasonality is named explicitly. In this market, Ramadan, Eid, DSF and the summer exodus move volumes enough to fake a churn signal. A model that flags every Ramadan dip as churn gets switched off in April.
  • Missing data stays missing. The score's denominator shrinks rather than the model imputing a number. An AM who catches the tool inventing one figure will never trust the other twelve.

What this is not

This is a 60-minute prototype on synthetic data. A real build would need: a trained churn model validated against actual historical churn rather than a hand-weighted rubric, back-testing on at least four quarters, an honest false-positive rate, and a feedback loop capturing which recommendations AMs acted on and which they ignored — that last one being the only thing that makes the weights better over time.

Prototype built for an application exercise. Synthetic data — no confidential or proprietary information. Single self-contained HTML file, no dependencies, no tracking.
